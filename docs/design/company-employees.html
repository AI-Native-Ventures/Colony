Company Employees

The agent is not its runtime. Settled 2026-08-05 after four rounds of design pressure; supersedes docs/design/role-agents.html.

The three laws

The workspace owns the role. Identity, prompt, rank, memory, and the job queue belong to the company, on the relay.
The seat owns the binding. Which harness and model execute an employee is each member's choice, on each member's machine, within each member's budget.
The human owns the consent. A subscription seat serves its own human; anything else is a per-task, human-approved handoff.

Who this is for

The primary persona is an under-resourced founding team, often in emerging markets. Their compute budget is subsidized consumer subscriptions (Claude Max, ChatGPT), not API credits. Seats are personal, quota-limited, and bannable for account sharing. Founders deliberately mix vendors to stretch budgets. Any design that requires API pricing, concentrates work on one founder's seat, or automates one member's seat for another's benefit fails this persona - the last one catastrophically, since a banned seat is an unaffordable loss.

The model

An employee is a workspace object: one keypair (minted and held by the relay at hire, never on a laptop), one memory, one queue of jobs, one rank in the interrupt hierarchy. A member's machine runs a worker, authenticated as the member, that claims jobs from employee queues and executes them through that member's binding. The result posts signed as the employee, stamped with who executed it, on what harness and model.

Why the identity key lives on the relay

Both members' machines must be able to produce output as the employee. A key copied to N laptops must be distributed and rotated on member exit - the trap that killed the shared-key design. A key held only by the relay has nothing to distribute and nothing to rotate; workers submit results as themselves and the relay signs as the employee. The relay already authors events in the company's name (stall asks, promotions in the interrupt system) and already holds the entire database; this is consistent custody, not new trust. These are protocol keys - Nostr identity - and have nothing to do with any vendor's credentials. Subscription tokens never leave the laptop they were created on.

Jobs, ownership, and the queue

A mention, DM, or delegation to an employee files a job: who asked (signed), which employee, which thread. Delegation chains inherit the originating human - the Chief of Staff tagging Sift on Basheer's behalf makes a job that is still Basheer's. The relay leases a job to exactly one worker; heartbeats release abandoned claims; a job nobody can claim waits visibly and escalates through the interrupt ladder to a human. Work either runs or becomes an ask in someone's inbox. It never silently dies.

Seat bindings

Each member configures, once: "on my machine, employees run on this harness and model, falling back to these." Per-employee overrides exist but are the exception. Quota exhaustion walks the fallback chain - degradation, not stoppage. The same employee may therefore produce different quality for different members; that is the founders' budget decision, made visible, never papered over: every execution stamp carries member, harness, and model, and the spend ledger prices it at that vendor's real rates.

Routing and the handoff

A member's worker claims only jobs their own human originated. Cross-seat execution exists solely as a handoff ask: "Basheer needs X run and his seat can't do it - run it on yours?" One tap, one task, executed by a human choosing to use their own account. Two triggers only: a seat whose whole fallback chain is exhausted, and credential-pinned roles (an employee holding one member's deploy token executes only on that member's machine). There is no standing setting that lets one member's jobs auto-run on another member's seat: a toggle like that is seat-sharing in effect and is the design's one hard prohibition. If handoffs ever become frequent, bounded revocable lane grants (modeled on the interrupt system's delegation grants) may be added - granted by the seat owner, narrow, visible, revocable.

What the employee knows

At claim time the worker assembles context from the relay: the thread, the employee's memory, and a workspace brief - the members, their roles (founder, co-founder), and who filed this job. The employee addresses people as who they are and knows whose task it is working, because the job record says so cryptographically, not because a prompt asserted it.

Private agents

Unchanged and default. An agent a member creates ad hoc is theirs: local key, local record, owner-only, invisible to the workspace. Hiring it into the company is the explicit act that mints a relay-held identity, publishes the role, and asks about any credentials it carries. Nothing a member makes becomes shared by accident.

The pattern across all seven: each coupled two concerns the final model separates. Complexity kills; the simple model - agent is not runtime - is the one that survived every pushback.

How this composes with the systems already shipping

  • Interrupt system (PR #86): ranks, typed asks, and escalation attach to the one employee identity. The handoff is a new ask type on that machinery. Stall detection is the queue's safety net.
  • Spend ledger (PRs #76-#85): multi-vendor pricing and conditional rates are exactly what per-seat bindings need; every execution stamp prices at the binding's real vendor rate, per cost centre.
  • Company blueprint (buzz-core company_roster): the hire flow and derived identities already exist for blueprint roles; "hire an existing agent" extends the same roster to ad-hoc agents.
  • Role-on-profile merging (0.10.8): handles the migration period and any transitional duplicates; the roster becomes the primary mechanism.

Build phases

  1. Employee registry and relay-held identity. built Hire mints the keypair relay-side; owner signs the ownership tag; employee record published to the workspace. Gate: a hired employee visible to both members, its events relay-signed. Passed against production: Chief of Staff hired on horizon, sealed key in the relay's database, the owner's request the only thing that authorised it.
  2. Job queue. built Kinds and relay logic: file, lease, heartbeat, complete, fail, stall-to-ask. Gate: a job filed by one member, claimed and completed by a worker, survives a mid-job worker kill by re-leasing. Passed against a live relay (crates/buzz-test-client/tests/e2e_jobs.rs). Three things only running it revealed: two heads written in the same second tie under NIP-33 and readers keep the stale one, two claims in the same second are one event and the retry vanishes, and a worker's pubkey does not identify its lease because one founder's laptop and desktop share a key. The queue carries a monotonic head stamp, a claim nonce, and an attempt fencing token because of those three.
  3. Worker mode and seat bindings. built Harness claims own-human jobs, executes through the member's binding with fallback chain, posts stamped results. Gate: the same employee completes jobs for both founders on different bindings, stamps and ledger correct. The stamp protocol and relay carriage are proven end to end (crates/buzz-test-client/tests/e2e_jobs.rs): heads carry the provider/model stamp and self-metered ledger records are readable by their author. The worker binding-selection path is not yet proven by an automated test: the E2E signs the finished jobs directly, so no seat config is loaded and worker.rs and llm.rs never run.
  4. Workspace briefs. Claim-time context: members, roles, requester identity, employee memory. Gate: the employee correctly addresses each founder and cites who filed the task, unprompted.
  5. Handoff asks and credential pinning. Exhausted-seat and pinned-role paths as typed asks. Gate: a job on an exhausted seat lands as an approvable ask on the other seat and runs only after the tap.
  6. Hire-existing-agent flow. Promote a private agent to employee: identity minted, credentials interrogated, respond-to flipped by category. Gate: Sift hired, both founders delegating to one Sift.
