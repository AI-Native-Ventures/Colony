# Colony landing page positioning proposal

Date: 2026-09-08
Status: Founder approved the direction and authorised completion. Implemented in the isolated hotfix/agency-landing-page worktree; publication is pending release checks. The wording below records the design direction; site source contains the final public copy.
Confirmed in this discussion: the primary action is **Apply for early access**.

## Copy acceptance rule

The founder requires a high school student with no prior knowledge of Colony to understand the page without explanation or hidden meaning. This is a comprehension requirement, not permission to change the intended buyer to students or use a childish tone.

Explain what Colony is before describing its team. Name the actual work and what the person does. Use concrete verbs such as write, create, check, change and publish. A short sentence still fails if the reader must decode it.

Do not rely on phrases such as “brief your teammate”, “keep client delivery moving”, “equipped job-based teammates”, “accountable AI team”, “social content” or “supported scope” in public copy. For example, replace “brief your teammate” with “Tell the AI assistant what you want it to make.” Replace “social content” with “pictures and words for Instagram posts.” Introduce job titles only alongside a description of what they do.

Every section must help a new visitor answer a practical question: What is this? Who is it for? What can it make? What do I do? What happens after I apply? Show a specific example instead of relying on an abstract benefit. Explain early access beside the button. Do not hide availability qualifications in a footer.

## Evidence and audience

Reviewed the live landing page at https://colony.ainative.ventures/ through its browser accessibility tree and viewport screenshots. Read the task “Define startup product positioning”, PRODUCT.md, and the September 7 launch recommendation. The task reader returned the earlier positioning conversation through the delegation discussion; the more recent launch decisions below are supported by the saved recommendation, rather than assumed to have appeared in that retrieved task history.

The founder's latest clarification governs this proposal: Colony is a flexible business platform, with the website specifically targeting digital marketing agencies that build/manage websites or design/manage social media content. There are two equally important visitors: entrepreneurs starting an agency and owners running an existing agency. The product supplies AI agents equipped to do the actual work, alongside client discovery across industries. The page must explain that whole proposition rather than reduce Colony to an individual content-generation tool.

The agency focus is the initial set of business types Colony directly caters for, not a permanent restriction on who may use it. Users may adapt the platform for other kinds of business; more ready-to-use business types are intended later. Explain this lower on the page without implying every business type is already supported out of the box. “Business tabs” in the spoken clarification is interpreted as business types, not a request to add navigation tabs.

Horizon Labs is a design partner and example business. Colony supplies the product those businesses use; Horizon's client services and R2,000 price are not Colony's subscription offer.

Customers should encounter an AI teammate equipped for a recognisable job, examples of what to ask, actual outputs, and a clear review process. Skills, models, routing, workers and technical configuration belong behind that experience. The proposed launch jobs are Social Media Manager and Website Manager. Social Media Manager's Instagram loop is first in the proof sequence. That sequence does not establish public availability of either complete job.

## What the live page communicates today

| Observed content | Problem for the intended buyer | Proposed change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero lists chat, agent teams, workflows, canvas and git | Describes software components before a useful result; does not name the first audience | Lead with starting or running a website/social media agency and explain the work its AI team can do |
| Staffed on day one, four setup steps, Scout, org chart and staffing proposal | Requires visitors to understand an AI organisation before seeing its value | Introduce the useful job and output first; compress coordination into one plain sentence |
| Discovery industries, prospect lists and an outreach approval example | Gives acquisition several sections without equally explaining the agency's services | Keep finding potential clients as a core benefit alongside website and social media work; compress repeated Discovery sections and separate discovery from outreach |
| New customers by morning | Confuses identified prospects with acquired customers and implies a time-bound commercial result | Remove; any future Discovery copy should say prospects and describe its actual scope |

The existing violet, ant identity and direct typography are worth retaining. The main change is information hierarchy. The observed hero devotes substantial space to branding and broad promises before showing a deliverable. Give concrete work more prominence; keep decorative ants away from body copy and actions.

## Approaches considered

1. **Start or run an agency, selected direction.** Explain finding potential clients, website creation/management and social media creation/management. Address first-time entrepreneurs and existing owners explicitly. Label early-access scope honestly.
2. **Broad AI team for any business.** Preserves the long-term ambition but makes it harder to explain the first useful job and creates a larger promise than the initial release.
3. **Instagram Social Media Manager only.** Most focused private-pilot proposition, but could make Colony look like a social scheduling product. Appropriate if the current recruitment offer is strictly social-only; broader jobs must be visibly future scope.

## Recommended page story

### 1. Recognise the visitor and the work

Suggested draft hero:

> Coming soon
>
> **Start or run your agency with an AI team.**
>
> Colony is an app for people who want to start an agency, or already run one, that builds websites or manages social media for other businesses.
>
> Find potential clients. Ask AI assistants to build and update websites, design social media posts and write the words that go with them.
>
> Tell them about your client and what you need done. They do the work, and you check it and ask for changes.
>
> **Apply for early access** · See an example
>
> Ask to try Colony before it opens to everyone.

This is draft copy for the intended agency proposition. At publication, explain exactly what selected applicants can try, directly beside the offer. If only social is available in the initial pilot, say so explicitly and label website functions as planned. Keep the start/run-agency positioning; do not silently reduce the whole product to Instagram because that is the first technical proof. Do not imply that applying grants immediate access.

Avoid the unqualified public phrase “everything you need to run a business.” Name the included work instead so readers do not infer accounting, contracts, payments, guaranteed customers or other unestablished services. “Ready to use” must describe the complete customer experience actually available, including any required connections.

### 2. Help both kinds of visitor recognise themselves

> **Starting an agency?**
> Offer website and social media services to businesses. Use Colony's AI assistants to help create the work you sell.
>
> **Already have an agency?**
> Give your AI team work for your clients, from building a website to preparing their next social media posts.

Follow with the three concrete capabilities below. Do not require a visitor to understand agency terminology without the explanation of selling websites or social media services to other businesses.

### 3. Explain the work the product helps them do

> **Find potential clients.**
> Choose the kind of business you want to work with, such as bakeries or guest houses. Find businesses to research and contact.
>
> **Build and look after websites.**
> Ask an AI assistant to create a client's website or update its pages, pictures and prices.
>
> **Create and manage social media posts.**
> Ask an AI assistant to design pictures, write posts and prepare a posting schedule for each client.

These are proposed descriptions of the offer. Verify the precise available creation, scheduling, publishing and website-management paths before publication. Explain supported networks and any handover/account-access needs in ordinary language. Discovery identifies potential clients; it does not guarantee they need the service, consent to outreach or become paying customers. Do not claim automatic outreach or reply handling from discovery alone.

### 4. Show one request becoming useful work

Use one consistent client example:

> Make three Instagram posts for my client, Green Street Bakery, about their new chocolate cake.

Example explanation: “The assistant prepares three pictures and the words for each post. You can look at them and ask for changes, such as ‘Make the writing shorter.’” Label the business and work as an illustrative example unless replaced with permissioned actual work. Show actual pictures and words alongside the explanation; do not substitute a status message saying they exist.

Show the brief, branded post previews and captions, then the review action. Show scheduled or published status only when supported by a real proven flow. Clearly label illustrative mockups as examples; do not present them as customer results or active software.

The visitor should see the actual work at a readable size. A dense screenshot of the whole app, a conversation claiming completion, or an org chart does not establish output quality.

### 5. Introduce the assistants through those jobs

Suggested public heading: **Help with social media posts.** Explanation: “Create pictures and write the words for your clients' Instagram posts.” Introduce Social Media Manager as the name of the AI assistant after the job is clear. State what applicants can actually try. Scheduling and publication need their own verified claims.

Suggested public heading: **Help with websites.** Explanation: “Create a website for a client, or update their pages, pictures and prices.” Example request: “Add the bakery's new opening hours to its website.” Introduce Website Manager after that explanation. Label this as planned until its own delivery path passes. Explain any access needed to update an existing site. Avoid implying arbitrary booking, commerce or hidden backend systems can be copied from a URL.

Omit the coordination explanation from the main story. The reader does not need to understand specialists or a chief-of-staff hierarchy to understand the job. A later optional explanation can say “You speak to one AI assistant. It can ask other AI assistants to help with the work.”

### 6. Explain the owner's part

Suggested public steps:

1. **Tell it what you need.** “Describe the business and what you want to make.”
2. **Look at the work.** “Open the pictures, writing or website it has prepared.”
3. **Ask for changes.** “Tell it what to change until you're ready to use the work.”

These steps describe the planned preparation and review experience, not a substitute for actual publishing. Explain the exact next step for approved work when its delivery path is confirmed. Avoid an unqualified “until you're happy” promise that implies unlimited revisions. Any promise about approvals, spend limits or recurring execution must match the actual product.

### 7. Explain the wider platform briefly

Suggested lower-page FAQ:

> **Can I use Colony for another kind of business?**
> You can adapt Colony for other kinds of work. We're starting with ready-to-use AI teams for website and social media agencies. We plan to add teams for more types of business.

This describes platform flexibility and direction. Confirm the concrete customisation path before publishing that answer. Do not use “open” without explanation: it could mean open-source, free, public signup or unrestricted access. Early access remains the confirmed conversion path; the founder's statement about openness does not silently change availability or pricing.

### 8. Resolve adoption questions and invite an application

Answer ordinary buyer questions: who early access is for, what is available, what they must provide, what remains their responsibility, how access works, and when cost will be disclosed. Do not import Horizon pricing, invent a free trial, guarantee savings, or promise a response deadline that has not been agreed.

Suggested initial application fields: name, email, “I'm starting an agency / I already run an agency”, and what help they need first. Business name and website should be optional so someone who has not started is not excluded. Explain what happens after submission in the actual operating process. Choose the destination, access and handling of applications before implementing collection. Do not publish a form until a submission can be received and processed reliably.

## Scope and acceptance gates

**Planning gate:** the founder has confirmed start/run-agency positioning, website and social media agencies as the initial audience, client discovery as part of the value, simple copy and early-access conversion. Review the revised wording, actual pilot availability labels and application questions. This copy remains draft; no application code has changed.

**Preview gate:** build a private preview using the existing site structure and brand. Update the hero, evidence, teammate introduction, owner workflow, FAQ and early-access section. Remove redundant org-chart, blocks and office-inventory sections from the primary story. Review actual desktop and phone layouts, readable outputs, keyboard access, reduced-motion behavior and application success/failure states.

**Comprehension gate:** have unfamiliar nontechnical people, including a reader at high-school level, explain what Colony is, whether they need an existing agency, which services it helps them sell, what they could ask it to make, what they will receive, what they must do, and what happens after applying. They should understand that finding a potential client is different from winning one. Ask them to explain it in their own words without coaching. Proposed target: at least four of five can answer after a short read. Any recurring misunderstanding requires a rewrite even if a readability score is low. This is a future acceptance test, not a completed study.

**Publication gate:** verify every capability and availability claim against the current product, confirm application handling and externally consequential terms, publish with founder authority, then verify the served page and an authorised test submission. Local preview, deployment and live submission proof are separate states.

## Final implementation decisions

Applications prepare a reviewable email addressed to basheer@ainative.ventures, confirmed by the founder. A copy/selectable-text fallback is provided. No form backend or new external provider was introduced. Preparing a draft never claims an application was received.

The page retains Colony's existing five-colour identity and ant mark. It shows one fictional bakery's website and social post with working example selectors, without presenting the examples as product proof. The initial offer is explicitly coming soon with limited early access.
