Two videos for people becoming users

"Start here" walks a new owner through setting up Colony on the real app. "What your AI team can and can't do" answers the fears that stop owners signing up. Both live where people already are (welcome email, colony.global, help), so nobody has to reply to comments.

Rules every frame follows

True to the app people download

  • Screens come from the real app (production, main), not drawings.
  • Every on-screen word is the app's own word.
  • Every claim has a proof line in the code, listed below.

No promises

  • Show the work, never results: no customers, sales or meetings promised.
  • No "free". No phone calls (not in the app).
  • Prices stay out of the voice; the app shows its own.

Written for strangers

  • Each line makes sense to a plumber who has never heard of Colony.
  • Same voice as the films (Aoede), big captions, calm music.
  • 16:9, 1920 x 1080. No em dashes anywhere.

How it's made

1CaptureA Playwright script drives the real app in its test mode with Bright Plumbing's sample data, the way the existing first-run, Welcome and first-job tests already do. At each beat it checks the screen's heading, then saves a sharp screenshot and the position of every button and field it will point at.
2ComposeTwo new compositions in video/, StartHere and CanCant: the app in a clean window on the brand gradient, the camera easing to what matters, a pointer that clicks the real button positions, a ring on the thing being explained, big captions, voice per beat, a calm music bed.
3CheckTests pin the beat timing (voice fits its screen, captions on screen long enough to read) and that every video 2 line has a proof entry. Then the usual gates: 1920 x 1080, -16 LUFS, no dark frames, the same frame renders the same twice.
4Re-makeWhen the app changes, run the capture again and re-render. A renamed screen fails the capture loudly instead of shipping an out-of-date tutorial.

The capture script lives with the app's tests but runs only on demand, never in CI. Screenshots are generated, not committed.
